ABOUT

Buildings, in human words.

The idea

Facade engineering hides behind vocabulary. Between the person who wants a building and the model that describes it stands a wall of CAD terms, layers and file formats — so every change travels through specialists, and every misunderstanding travels to the factory.

GFP turns that around. You speak the way people speak about buildings — floors, groups of floors, this one stands back, make the rhythm looser — and the engine owns the geometry: grids, mullions, sheets, trays. The words stay human; the millimetres stay exact.

What stands today

A working engine, in daily use: conversational building from a plain brief; live elevations, plans, sections and a street view; engineering truth — glass sheets within fabrication limits, unified stacks, drainage trays at every broken junction, wind pressure to SI 414; DWG and JSON exports; and a signed-in workshop reachable over the internet. It is operated today by its author — the multi-user cloud workshop is the road ahead, and this site is its front door.

Three principles

Nothing without your yes. Every change is asked as a plain question before it is applied, and one undo always stands ready.

Every step on the record. The engine writes a protocol as it works — what was ordered, what was built, what was refused — so a disagreement is settled by reading, not by arguing.

Refusals speak. When something cannot be done, the engine names the reason and the exact door that fixes it. A silent failure is treated as a bug of the highest order.

FOR INVESTORS

The facade line, rebuilt.

The problem

Custom curtain walls are still engineered largely by hand: weeks of drawings for every option, knowledge locked in a few specialists, and errors that surface at the factory or on the crane — the most expensive places to find them. The result is a market where exploring a better facade costs so much that most buildings never try.

The approach

GFP holds the entire facade as one live model and puts a conversation in front of it. An owner or architect speaks in floors and groups; an AI operator works the engine; the engine computes deterministic geometry and refuses honestly what cannot be built. Drawings, schedules and exports fall out of the same model, so they cannot disagree — and an option that used to cost a week costs a sentence.

What exists today

A working prototype in daily use by its author: conversational building from a plain brief, live drawings, fabrication-aware glass schedules, wind checks to SI 414, drainage resolution at broken junctions, DWG and JSON exports, an approval-gated write path with a full protocol, and a password-protected workshop reachable over the internet. A live demonstration is available on request.

Where it goes

From one machine to a cloud engine; from one author to seats for architects, facade contractors and owners; from a single building to a library of engineered systems. The near-term road is unglamorous and concrete: hosted engines, accounts, and the first outside users working real projects.
